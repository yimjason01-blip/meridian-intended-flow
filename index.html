Meridian intended onboarding flow

Collect the baseline once. Use the visit to decide.

Meridian should enter the first physician conversation with enough context to make decisions, not with a list of missing inputs. Intake shapes the baseline, collection happens once, synthesis happens before the visit, and cadence becomes the product.

Core change

One baseline event, not a testing maze.

Mobile venous collection is the default. Capillary kits are useful later for narrow rechecks, not as the front door.

1

Frame the patient

Structured intake, prior records, goals, family history, meds, and wearable backfill.

Output: clinical context.
2

Plan the baseline

Universal foundation panel plus intake-triggered add-ons across major risk domains.

Output: collection plan.
3

Collect once

Mobile phlebotomy handles the baseline draw. Imaging and function run only when they affect decisions.

Output: complete baseline.
4

Synthesize first

Labs, imaging, genetics, wearables, function, and records become a physician pre-read.

Output: open decisions.
5

Decide cadence

The visit produces actions, orders, monitoring buckets, and risk-tiered recheck intervals.

Output: durable care rhythm.
Supporting rails
Genetics runs in parallel.

It informs surveillance and interpretation. It should not block the blood draw.

Imaging and function are risk-gated.

DEXA, CAC, CPET, grip, or MRI belong when they change the first conversation.

Capillary kits move to follow-up.

Tasso-style collection is valuable for narrow rechecks after the baseline is known.

What changed from the old flow

No split baseline across a home capillary kit and a separate lab visit. The first pass should feel coordinated.

What the first visit is for

Decisions: what to act on, what to watch, and what not to over-medicalize.

What makes this defensible

The baseline is complete enough for decisions, but cadence prevents one-time testing theater.
